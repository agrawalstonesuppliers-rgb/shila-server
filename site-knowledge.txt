=== STONES SUPPLIED ===

1. KOTA LIMESTONE (the core product)
- Fine-grained calcareous limestone, quarried and cut at Amarpura.
- Colours: Grey/blue-grey, green, brown & honey.
- Density 2,400–2,600 kg/m3. Compressive strength 1,800–2,200 kg/cm2.
  Modulus of rupture 170–200 kg/cm2. Water absorption under 0.40%. Mohs hardness 3–4.
- Excellent frost/weather resistance, non-slip natural face. Acid-sensitive — avoid strong
  acids on polished faces.
- GRADES (quality sorting — independent of finish): Fresh (tightest sort, even colour, clean
  edges — for visible interior floors/showrooms), Medium (wider tolerance, more tonal
  variation, cheaper — for back-of-house/budget work), Waterproof (rough-reject grade,
  rough face ONLY — roofs, sheds, plinths, paths).
- IMPORTANT: grade and finish are separate choices. Fresh and Medium are each available in
  ANY finish (rough natural, diamond-sawn, honed, mirror polish, table polish, high polish).
  Waterproof is the one exception — rough face only, not offered in other finishes.
- Thickness supplied: 11–102 mm.
- Best for: residential/commercial flooring, corridors, courtyards, covered walkways. Not
  recommended for continuously wet external areas without a compatible sealant.

2. MANDANA SANDSTONE (Red)
- Hard, close-grained natural red sandstone, denser than most Indian sandstones.
- Colour: red, deepening to warm red-brown under polish.
- Density 2,200–2,400 kg/m3. Compressive strength 800–1,200 kg/cm2. Modulus of rupture
  120–160 kg/cm2. Water absorption 1.0–2.0%. Mohs hardness 6–7 (quartz-bearing).
- Excellent weather resistance, through-body colour (doesn't fade). Very good acid/alkali
  resistance.
- Thickness: 15–100 mm. Finishes: natural, honed, mirror, diamond polish.
- Best for: building elevations, wall cladding, exposed paving, garden paths, boundary work.

3. KANDLA GREY SANDSTONE
- Hard, quartz-bearing sandstone. Pale silver-grey dry, deepens to mid grey wet/sealed.
- Density 2,200–2,400 kg/m3. Compressive strength 1,100–1,600 kg/cm2. Modulus of rupture
  120–150 kg/cm2. Water absorption 0.5–2.0%. Porosity 3–5%. Mohs hardness 6–7.
- Excellent frost resistance (widely used in European paving). High slip resistance in
  riven/sandblasted/flamed finishes.
- Thickness: 15–100 mm. 9 finishes available.
- Best for: external paving, courtyards, pool surrounds, landscaping.

4. RAJ GREEN SANDSTONE
- Hard, silica-based sandstone. Pale cream-buff sawn/sandblasted; multi-tonal green/buff/
  brown/grey in natural riven face. Through-body colour, doesn't fade.
- Density 2,200–2,400 kg/m3. Compressive strength 1,000–1,500 kg/cm2. Modulus of rupture
  120–160 kg/cm2. Water absorption 1.0–3.0%. Porosity 3–6%. Mohs hardness 6–7.
- Excellent frost resistance.
- Thickness: 15–100 mm. 8 finishes available.
- Best for: landscaping, garden paving, patios, external walkways.

5. BLACK BASALT
- Fine-grained igneous volcanic rock (Deccan Traps). Dense, non-porous, ~3x the compressive
  strength of Kota limestone.
- Colour: jet black to charcoal grey, deepens when polished.
- Density 2,800–3,000 kg/m3. Compressive strength 1,500–3,000 kg/cm2 (150–300 MPa).
  Modulus of rupture 200–250 kg/cm2 (20–25 MPa). Water absorption under 0.5%. Mohs hardness 6.
- Excellent frost & salt resistance (coastal/de-iced surfaces OK). Excellent acid/alkali
  resistance (chemically inert). Very high slip resistance when flamed/bush-hammered.
- Thickness: 15–100 mm. 9 finishes available.
- Best for: high-traffic commercial paving, coastal/poolside surfaces, cladding, pharma/
  industrial/cleanroom surrounds (maximum compressive strength + chemical inertness).

=== CITY-SPECIFIC NOTES (from each city's dedicated supplier page) ===
Each of these 25 cities has its own full page (kota-stone-supplier-<city>.html) with buying
patterns, timing and specification advice. Key points per city, if a visitor asks about a
specific city:

- Delhi/NCR: heaviest market for polished 22"×22" Kota (builder floors, stair treads, lift
  lobbies); grey Kota 20–25mm calibrated is standard; Mandana red for elevation/temple.
  Winter is peak buying season — book 6–8 weeks ahead of a November start.
- Jaipur: buys fresh & medium Kota in volume for economy flooring; Mandana red for elevation.
  Close enough that buyers can visit the yard and select stock in person before cutting.
- Mumbai/Pune: specifies harder & thinner — calibrated 18–20mm mirror-polish for thin-bed
  high-rise work; basalt (not porous Kota grades) for external/coastal salt-spray areas.
  Pune: basalt is also quarried locally in Maharashtra — worth comparing delivered rate vs
  local basalt.
- Ahmedabad/Surat: Gujarat is the main export route (Mundra/Kandla/Hazira ports) — mention
  at quotation stage if consignment is ultimately for export, packing differs. Mandana red
  heavy for elevation/temple; Kandla Grey for external paving/township landscaping.
- Bengaluru: commercial/campus flooring (corridors, basements) where stone beats vitrified
  tile on life-cycle cost; freight-sensitive so thickness discipline matters — told honestly
  where 20mm suffices vs not.
- Hyderabad: price-sensitive, well-informed buyers who ask precise grade/calibration
  questions. Always confirm grade (fresh/medium) in writing — this is where most disputes
  start if not specified clearly.
- Chennai: "granite country" — Kota sells on cost/look of honed limestone rather than
  hardness. Coastal humidity/salt air — basalt or dense green Kota recommended over porous
  grades for external work.
- Kolkata: institutional/heritage-adjacent work, Mandana for restoration/temple projects.
  Monsoon timing matters more here than any other market — plan delivery window around it.
- Lucknow/Kanpur/Varanasi (UP belt): strong Mandana red market for elevation/temple/heritage
  work; grey Kota for residential floors. UP is price-sensitive with many intermediaries —
  grade misdescription is a known risk, always confirm grade. Varanasi specifically: temple/
  ghat/hospitality work, thicker sections than average since much is permanently wet and
  heavily walked externally.
- Indore/Bhopal: Indore — strong honed/mirror-polish Mandana and brown Kota market, repeat
  smaller lots preferred, low freight means thicker stone (30–40mm) is economical for
  driveways. Bhopal — government/institutional tenders (schools, hospitals) specifying grey
  Kota 25mm honed; tenders usually need test certificates — mention the tender spec so one
  batch can be reserved.
- Chandigarh: well-finished stone market — calibrated mirror/diamond-polish for lobbies;
  Punjab buys heavy Mandana red for gurdwara/farmhouse work. Real freeze risk in some
  pockets — ask before specifying external paving.
- Dehradun: institutional/hospitality plus heavy temple/ghat work at Haridwar/Rishikesh.
  Freeze-thaw is a genuine issue in hill districts — steer away from risk-prone specs at
  altitude.
- Agra: red sandstone country by tradition — Mandana red for elevation/temple/restoration
  work; grey Kota for hotel/institutional floors. Tone-matching matters more than usual when
  a job sits alongside existing red sandstone.
- Ajmer/Kishangarh: Kishangarh is India's marble trading capital — dealers increasingly
  stock Kota alongside marble; ask about standing orders if you're a Kishangarh dealer.
  Ajmer itself: institutional/religious work (dargah, temple, school) in 25–32mm grey Kota.
- Jodhpur: local sandstone is cheap there, so what moves is what local beds don't supply —
  grey/green Kota for hard-wearing floors, black basalt for external paving, calibrated
  polished Kota for commercial fit-outs.
- Nagpur/Raipur (Vidarbha/Chhattisgarh): hard, plain fresh/medium grey Kota for institutional
  and government work, generally 25mm, ground in place rather than factory polished. Nagpur
  summers are severe with compressed site programmes — book ahead of monsoon-end starts.
  Raipur: steel-belt industrial/institutional; freight is the single biggest cost line — a
  full trailer load materially beats a part load on delivered rate.
- Patna: grey Kota in volume for housing; Mandana red for temple/ghat/elevation. Long random
  slabs (not cut-to-size squares) move well due to traditional laying practice. Order in dry
  months — monsoon stretches lead times and stresses roads.
- Udaipur: hospitality market first (hotels/havelis/resorts) — Mandana red for elevation/
  courtyard, honed or in-situ-polished grey Kota for heritage-style circulation floors.
  It's marble country too, so expect to be quoted against marble.
